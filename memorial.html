Shoah Victims

Members of our family who were murdered in the Shoah, and others who died during the German occupation of Ukraine. This page lists only people who are no longer living. Where the records do not settle who someone was, we say so.

May the memory of the Righteous be for a blessing.
השם יקום דמו

Teplyk, 27 May 1942.

Before dawn, German soldiers and local auxiliary police drove about 800 Jews from the Teplyk ghetto, most of them women, children and the elderly, to the former soap factory on the edge of town and shot them. Yad Vashem's record of the site is here, and the history of Teplyk and Zhabokrych is on the towns page.

Yad Vashem's records name some of the people listed below as having died in Teplyk in May 1942, probably in this massacre.

A survivor's account

Sof'ia Moiseevna Palatnikova (1927–2021) of Teplyk was interviewed by the Archive of Historical Eastern Yiddish Memories (AHEYM) at Indiana University.

Palatnik lineDaughter of Moshe PalatnikGranddaughter of Pincus (Philip) PalatSister of Tatiana Marinina (1921–2010)

Sof'ia was born in Teplyk on 28 December 1927. Her father was a butcher. In the 1930s the family lived on the Lunacharskii Jewish collective farm in Crimea, and she went to school for six years before the war interrupted her education. She survived the war in Teplyk and Bershad and in the camps at Bratslav, Haysyn and Raygorod. After the war she worked in an industrial complex for twenty-two years.

In this clip she describes how her family tried to flee Teplyk before the Germans took the town on 26 July 1941, but waited too long: "as soon as they attacked, they were already here." The Germans caught up with them and made them return. She goes on to describe the ghetto, where Jews cleared snow from the roads and wore armbands with the Star of David.

Video: watch on YouTube · Interview page: AHEYM, interview 1321. Courtesy of AHEYM, Indiana University.

Sof'ia and her sister Tatiana also filled out the Yad Vashem Pages of Testimony for people who died in Teplyk in May 1942, including the records for Lev, Yefim and Feyga listed above. On the form they described the people as fellow townspeople.

More names to research

Yad Vashem's Shoah Names Database has many more people named Palatnik and Vishnyak (in several spellings) who lived in Teplyk. We have not yet linked these to our tree, so they are not listed above. Each search returns the original Pages of Testimony, which are often the only surviving record of a person.
